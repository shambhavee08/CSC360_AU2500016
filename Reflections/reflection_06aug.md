## Reflection

This session helped me understand that computer graphics involves much more than simply creating visually appealing images. There is an entire process behind how images are represented, stored and manipulated by a computer. One concept that helped me understand this better was the representation of digital images through pixels. I learned that an image can be viewed as a collection of small individual elements, each carrying information that contributes to the overall appearance of the image.

One of the concepts I found particularly useful was the difference between raster and vector graphics. Before this discussion, I generally thought of digital images as a single category. However, understanding how these two formats work made me realise that the way an image is created and stored depends on its purpose. Raster graphics are made up of pixels and are commonly used for photographs, whereas vector graphics use mathematical descriptions of shapes and are useful for things such as logos and diagrams. This also helped me understand why enlarging certain images can make them appear blurry or pixelated, while vector graphics can be resized without losing their clarity.

Another interesting part of the session was learning about geometric transformations. Concepts such as translation, scaling and rotation helped me understand how objects can be moved, resized and repositioned within a graphical environment. I found it easier to relate to these concepts when thinking about practical examples, such as moving a triangle using mouse interactions or changing its size through zooming. It made me realise that mathematical operations are not just theoretical calculations but can directly control what we see and interact with on a screen.

I also gained some understanding of how Java can be used to develop graphical applications. The introduction to components such as JFrame, JPanel and the Graphics class gave me an idea of how different elements come together to create a graphical interface. What I found interesting was that even seemingly simple visual operations require specific instructions and calculations within the program.

The discussion about finding intersections and drawing perpendicular lines further highlighted the connection between mathematics and computer graphics. I realised that creating accurate graphical representations often requires an understanding of coordinates, angles and geometric relationships. This made me appreciate how mathematical concepts contribute to the functioning of applications that we generally interact with without thinking about the processes behind them.

Overall, this session gave me a better understanding of the relationship between mathematics, programming and visual representation. My biggest takeaway was that computer graphics is not simply about what appears on the screen; it is also about the logic and calculations that make those visuals possible. I would like to explore geometric transformations and their implementation in Java further, as understanding how mathematical operations translate into visible changes made this topic particularly interesting to me.
